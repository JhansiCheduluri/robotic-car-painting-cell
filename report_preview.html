1. Project Overview & Core Objectives

This technical report documents the design, kinematics modeling, collision-free trajectory planning, and surface coverage validation of an automated robotic car spray-painting booth. Using the RoboDK simulation engine and Python-driven kinematics generation, the cell orchestrates dual 6-axis articulated industrial painting robots operating in tandem with an auxiliary linear rail to achieve high-precision, uniform surface coating across complex automotive CAD bodywork.

3. Toolpath Planning & Kinematic Discretization

Surface coverage is accomplished via automated raster scanning toolpaths generated algorithmically in Python. The car geometry is segmented into 7 distinct topological surfaces: Hood, Roof, Trunk, Front Bumper, Rear Bumper, Right Flank, and Left Flank. Each surface is discretized into parallel raster strokes with strict orientation constraints:

  • Surface Normal Orientation: Tool +Z vector points strictly antiparallel to the surface local normal (-normal) at every waypoint to guarantee perpendicular spray impingement.
  • Smooth Approach & Retract Transitions: Prior to firing the spray trigger, the robot executes a linear approach along the tool vector from an offset standoff distance of 150 mm at 800 mm/s.
  • Dual-Robot Partitioning: Workpiece zones are split between Robot Left and Robot Right according to regional proximity, eliminating wrist singularities and joint limits.

6. Safety Interlocks & Collision Avoidance

Industrial painting cells feature high kinetic energy arms operating close to delicate vehicle skins. Safety is enforced through a multi-tier algorithmic interlock:

🔍 Continuous Collision Engine
Monitors contact pairs between Robot L, Robot R, linear rails, floor plates, and the automotive body in 1 ms simulation steps.
🚧 Obstacle Envelopes
Strict 60 mm safety buffer applied around windshield ($X=900$ mm) and rear window ($X=-1000$ mm) boundaries.
🛑 Pre-Motion Interlock
Identified 4 potential approach collision paths and safely aborted them prior to motion execution: 0 physical collisions occurred.

9. Failure Mode Analysis (Skipped Pieces)

Out of 204 planned trajectory pieces, exactly 4 pieces were selectively skipped by the proactive safety interlock:

  • Trunk (Piece 28): Robot Left attempted approach move at Lane 1600, Stop -1350 with Roll 90°. Flagged clearance warning against trunk lid lip; safely aborted without contact.
  • Roof (Pieces 100, 101, 102): Robot Right reached boundary proximity near upper windshield Pillar A. The pre-motion planner prevented joint limit saturation.
